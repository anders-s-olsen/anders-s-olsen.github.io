abstract: Instantaneous phase-locking (iPL) matrices, defined by the cosines of pairwise
  phase differences between band-limited signals, underpin widely used methods for
  time-resolved functional connectivity, including Leading Eigenvector Dynamics Analysis
  (LEiDA). Each iPL matrix has rank at most two and is fully characterized by its
  two nonzero eigenvalues and corresponding eigenvectors. Yet, existing methods retain
  different parts of this decomposition and compare them using distances that do not
  reflect the geometry of the underlying sample space. We show that weighting the
  two eigenvectors by the square roots of their eigenvalues recovers each channel's
  phase relative to the mean phase, and that optimal orthogonal Procrustes alignment
  of these weighted eigenvector pairs yields the Bures-Wasserstein distance between
  the corresponding iPL matrices. In EEG, where most channels oscillate nearly in
  phase, existing distances become insensitive to phase-lag size, whereas the Bures-Wasserstein
  distance preserves this sensitivity. In simulations, clustering with this distance
  recovered states differing in both lag size and spatial pattern that existing distances
  failed to distinguish.In resting-state EEG, it resolved distinct phase-gradient
  states that other methods merged into a single global in-phase state.
