abstract: Many signal-processing objects are defined by periodicity, normalization,
  sign or global-phase ambiguity, basis invariance, or positive-semidefinite constraints
  and therefore cannot be modeled faithfully in ordinary Euclidean space. This paper
  develops a geometry driven framework for directional statistics in signal processing,
  connecting signal representations to their natural manifolds or quotient spaces
  and to probability models compatible with their invariances. After reviewing instances
  of directional statistics in the signal processing literature, we review circular,
  toroidal, spherical, projective, Stiefel, Grassmann, and covariance-valued distributions,
  emphasizing their statistical assumptions, computational requirements, and dimensional
  limitations relevant to signal processing. We describe various modeling and estimation
  strategies, including analytical and gradient-based maximum likelihood, structured
  low-rank parameterizations, mixtures and hidden Markov models (HMMs), score-based
  and contrastive estimation, and geometry-aware K-means. To exemplify the consequences
  of geometry-aware modeling, we study multivariate phase coherence in synthetic data
  and functional neuroimaging, comparing toroidal phase vectors to projective, Grassmann,
  and positive-semidefinite representations. We also study speaker separation in a
  car environment, showing that directional representations outperform a standard
  Euclidean model. These examples demonstrate that when the geometric representation,
  statistical distribution, inference method, and computational regime are considered
  together, they have the potential to lead to improved models across a broad range
  of signal processing applications.
